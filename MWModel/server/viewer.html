The answer, at the horizons that mean anything

The current state is the part we already know. This is the part we want: the same world run forward many times, reported where inertia still rules — a week, a month, a quarter. There is deliberately no intraday column: nothing in this mechanism knows what oil does this afternoon. The annual horizon is run on demand (python -m mwmodel.project 365) because it costs a minute.

Circles are countries, sized by oil consumption and shaded by the inflation they are currently printing. Lines are seaborne crude leaving a producer through a strait; they thin and fade as a valve narrows. Click a valve to change it.

The valves

A blockade is not a special case here — it is a capacity that has fallen. What cannot pass tries the bypass and pays for the detour; only what fits through neither is stranded.

Where it is going

The same world run forward many times with the strait's future sampled, the elasticity uncertain and the residual resampled. The band is the 10th to 90th percentile; the solid line is the median. Everything left of the divider already happened.

Who pays for it

The same barrel produces a different consumer price in every country: different basket weights, different fuel taxes, different subsidies. That is the whole reason this is modelled per country rather than per planet.

    The chain of consequence
    
    
    Nothing here is wired country by country. A price changes, it is published
      to a channel, and whoever is subscribed to that channel hears it and republishes what it
      decided — crude reaches a refiner, diesel and bunker reach the shipping segments,
      a freight rate reaches the countries whose goods travel on that lane and nobody else.
      This is the cascade that actually ran on the last tick, in order, with what it descended
      from.

    Has any of this ever been right?
    
    
    The same world replayed from ten past dates, seeded only with what had been
      published on the day each run begins, and scored against what crude actually did. The
      null is a flat line — "tomorrow is today" — which for oil is a genuinely hard benchmark.
      Each window is run twice: told nothing more, and told the dated events as they happened.
      A ratio below 1.00 beats the flat line.

    Phase 0. The world turns, conservation holds, and the chain from a strait to a
    consumer price runs end to end — but every input is an order-of-magnitude placeholder and
    every coefficient is a starting value, not a finding. Real energy balances arrive in
    phase 1, real CPI baskets in phase 2. Nothing on this page is a forecast yet; it is a
    mechanism you can argue with. This is not a trading system and has no path to any
    exchange, wallet or key.
