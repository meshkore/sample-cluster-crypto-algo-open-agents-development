# System 10 — approach audit (2026-10-15)

Operator's question: is the architecture and the approach the best we can do; is the training
data enough (more coins, more candles, other markets, commodities, public data); would more
GPUs help; propose changes only where the gain is certain. Read against the code
(`search.py`, `tools/conditions.py`, `bandit.py`), the ledger (17,951 trials, 43 releases) and
the literature. Verdict first, detail below.

## Verdict

1. **The approach is sound and matches the evidence.** Gate + small selector nets + a next-day
   forecaster + a rule book is what the literature says survives in a low signal-to-noise market
   (Gu–Kelly–Xiu 2020: shallow nets beat deep ones in return prediction; Israel–Kelly–Moskowitz
   2020). The RL lines were closed on 10-14 for the right reason. The public page still
   describing "reinforcement learning" is stale text, not the system.
2. **The numbers we select on are inflated by two defects we can fix with certainty.** They do
   not make the system better; they make the backtest tell the truth about it, which today it
   does not. Both are measurable in our own ledger (section 2).
3. **More data: only cross-sectional breadth, and only point-in-time.** Everything else
   (longer history, equities/commodities as features, sentiment, on-chain) is either already in
   the exit model, refuted in this lab, or unsupported by the literature at our horizon.
4. **More GPUs: no.** The models are 2-layer MLPs that fit in minutes. Compute buys more trials,
   and more trials under the current selection buy more selection noise, not more edge.

## 1. What the system is today (release 43, live on the paper book since 10-15 09:02 UTC)

Per 15-minute bar, 14 USDT pairs, long only. Gate: BTC vs its 100-day average, breadth ≥ 0.5 in
the rising regime, open in the falling one at 0.75× stake. Selector: 4 seeds × MLP(128,128) on
the 44 price features + 1h/4h/1d/1w views + lagged macro panel, Huber loss, 6 epochs, 3-of-4 vote,
take-bar chosen on year N−1. Exit: 4 MLPs forecasting the next 96-bar return, clipped ±30%;
enter above +0.3%, keep above −0.3%, stake ∝ forecast. Book: 8 slots, 06's 16.3% stop, trail,
768-bar cap, take profit 20%, 2× stake on the top-5 coins by 90-day return.

Note: release 43 carries **neither the 12% stop nor the drawdown-scaled stakes** that the
decision log credits with making DD ≤ 25% reachable (`dd_scale` and `stop` are absent from its
cfg, so the book falls back to 06's 16.3% stop and no scaling). Its 25% cap is in-sample only.
Worth knowing before it holds real money.

## 2. The two certain defects

### 2.1 Survivor-selected universe (look-ahead in the asset list)

`system006_oracle_net_15m/universe.py` builds the 14 coins from Binance's **24-hour turnover
today**, status TRADING today, ≥ 540 days of history today. Every coin in the backtest is one
that was known in 2026 to have survived and stayed liquid. A trader in 2021 could not know that.

Why this matters more for S10 than for 06: the momentum boost (`mom_rank`: 2× stake on the top-5
by 90-day return) and the selector both rank coins **within a list of survivors**; momentum
measured on survivors is inflated because the coins that went to zero after a strong run are
not in the list. The A66 study in system 06 already showed the mechanism from the other side:
late-listed names with thin history destroyed the wide universe's 2019–2020 years.

Fix (certain to make the numbers honest, not certain to make them higher; expect lower): a
**point-in-time universe**. For each test year N, the eligible coins are those that on
31 December of N−1 had ≥ 540 days of history and ≥ the turnover floor measured on the trailing
30 days of candles on disk, including coins later delisted (Binance's data archive keeps klines
for delisted pairs). Rank, select and trade only among them. Candle data for the extra coins is
the only cost.

### 2.2 Selecting the maximum of 17,951 trials and calling 2022–2025 "unseen"

The years are unseen by the **models** (walk-forward, correct) but not by the **configuration**:
the release is the trial with the highest law score over the four years, picked from 6,686
eligible trials. That is one more max-selection on top of the fit, the same structure the
system 06 selection-optimism note documented. Our own ledger shows how little the top is worth:

| measurement (ledger, 2026-10-15) | value |
|---|---|
| eligible trials | 6,686 (6,565 distinct configurations) |
| law score: 1st / 5th / 20th / 100th | 0.272 / 0.271 / 0.266 / 0.249 |
| same configuration, second seed set: per-year return change | median 7.7 pp, p75 16.7 pp, p90 30 pp |

The top-100 configurations sit within 0.023 of score; a seed swap alone moves a single year by
8–30 points. Releases 31 → 33 → 43 in two days are moves inside that band. The confirm seed set
controls seed noise, not configuration selection. Release 20 (did not reproduce) and release 38
(one +92% year) were this defect showing itself.

Fix (certain, textbook): **nest the selection.** Choose the configuration on years < N with the
same law, then read N; the search's own output is walk-forwarded exactly as the models are.
Report that "chosen-on-the-past" curve as the system's expected performance; the current
max-over-trials curve becomes a research diagnostic. Alongside, compute the Probability of
Backtest Overfitting (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu 2017: CSCV over the trial ledger) and
the Deflated Sharpe (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) weekly in `review`; refuse a release whose
PBO > 0.5. Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016) is why a t-stat under 3 is noise after this many trials.
And a **release hysteresis** for the paper book: swap only when the nested score beats the
incumbent by more than the measured seed-noise band, never on a 0.001 gain.

## 4. Compute

The selector and exit fit in minutes on the 4060; a trial is bounded by the book loop, not by
the GPU. Ten more GPUs would run ten times more trials through the same max-selection, which
section 2.2 shows raises the headline and not the live result. Where compute IS worth spending:
the nested walk-forward of the search (each N re-runs the selection on < N: about 4× today's
cost), CSCV/PBO over the ledger (CPU), and the point-in-time universe (more coins, more rows).
All of it fits the current box in days; no new hardware is justified by the evidence.

## 5. Proposal, in order; no code until the operator says so

1. Point-in-time universe, deep history (section 2.1); then re-book releases 31 and 43 on it and
   publish the honest per-year table beside the current one.
2. Nested selection + PBO / Deflated Sharpe in `review` + release hysteresis (section 2.2).
3. Only then widen to 24–35 coins (section 3, row 1) and A/B funding/OI and taker share.

Expected effect: the headline CAGR falls (probably from +47% toward the +20–30% band the nested
curve will show), the worst year gets closer to zero, and the live book stops being surprised.
That is the gain: a system whose backtest predicts its live result.

## 6. Why reinforcement learning is not the better tool here (operator's question, 2026-10-15)

RL earns its keep where (a) an action changes the state the next action sees and (b) the
outcome of a sequence is only known at the end, so credit must be assigned backwards. Neither
holds for us. A $50k book does not move the market, and the outcome of every possible trade at
every bar is already on the tape. So every opportunity can be labelled with what it would have
earned and fitted at once (supervised); RL has to discover the same quantity from sampled
rewards, one episode at a time, where each 15-minute reward is noise around the 0.3% toll. It
is the same estimator with far more variance. That is what happened on 09-27 and 10-08: the
agent learned only that trading costs money (churn, then silence), or memorised the one path it
was given (2025 swings −26%..+14% between releases while a trend rule held +15%).

"Self-learning in a simulator" works when the simulator is the truth (Go, chess, robotics with
physics). Our only simulator is the past tape; replaying it creates no new information, and a
generated market path is a model of the market, not the market. Eight years, one realisation,
non-stationary: a policy with thousands of parameters fits that path. The literature agrees:
deep-RL trading results mostly fail to replicate, and the ones that hold (Zhang, Zohren,
Roberts 2020) add little over vol-scaled momentum.

What the condition search does is the thing the operator wanted from RL: it searches
conditions, levers and crosses, keeps what wins walk-forward, and never stops. It is not that
RL was badly done; it was the wrong estimator for a problem whose answers are already written
down. RL would only come back for a decision whose action changes the state: execution with
market impact at a size we do not trade.

## 7. Who manages the money, and can the pieces grow separately (operator, 2026-10-15)

They already are two layers, and the code treats them so: `search.signals()` caches the signal
layer independently of the money-management keys, and trials that differ only in the book reuse
the signals (`levers_study.py` A/Bs book levers on cached release inputs in minutes).

| layer | module | what it decides | how it is chosen today |
|---|---|---|---|
| signal | `search.py` gate, selector, exit regressor | when the market is tradeable, which opportunity, a continuous next-day forecast per bar | fitted walk-forward on years < N |
| book (money management) | `tools/conditions.py: book_year` | slots; stake = slot x forecast strength (25-100%) x regime (0.75 when BTC below its MA) x drawdown factor x momentum rank; every 4 h add a quarter slot if forecast >= +0.6%, halve if < 0; per-trade stop, trail, take profit, max hold; optional correlation cap, equity filter, account brake, vol target | rule levers, searched by the SAME max-over-trials score as the signals |

So the money manager exists, is deterministic, and already does entry sizing, scaling in and out
during the life of a position, and the exit (forecast + safety rules). What is missing is not a
module; it is that the book is chosen through the same selection noise as the signals (section
2.2), so a lever "wins" on 0.001 of score.

What can still be done in this layer, in order of evidence:
1. **Give the book its own honest test.** Fix the signal layer (a release), then choose the
   book levers on years < N and read N. Cheap (book-only re-runs), removes the joint selection.
2. **A sizer model (meta-labelling, Lopez de Prado 2018):** a second model that reads the
   state and the forecast and outputs P(this trade wins); stake ∝ that probability. System 06
   tested it: a drawdown reducer, not a return engine. Worth one honest A/B on S10's cached signals.
3. **Portfolio-level volatility targeting** (Moreira & Muir 2017; Harvey et al. 2018): today
   `vol_target` scales each coin; the book's total exposure is not targeted. Published to raise
   Sharpe and cut drawdowns in trend-following books; a one-lever addition.
4. **Execution**: fills are assumed at the 15-minute close at a 0.30% round trip. The BingX
   mirror will measure the real slippage; the book should read that number, not a constant.

Answer to the question: yes, the system is already two pieces that can be optimised apart, and
the right order is signals first (sections 2-3), then the book on frozen signals (this section).
Adding pieces is not hard; adding them without their own out-of-sample test is what hurts.
